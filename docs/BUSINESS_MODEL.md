# Gummy Business Model

## Principle

The personal computer is the adoption surface. Governance is the commercial product.

Gummy should not need to extract rent from every consumer file, model message, social relationship, or application. Individuals can run a compatible Gummy, choose their model, keep local data, and fork the shell.

## Paying customers

### Software vendors

Pay for Application Pack design, capability and workflow mapping, verification harnesses, compatibility certification, signed distribution, model/version regression testing, support, and incident investigation.

### Enterprises

Pay for identity and role control, policy engine, model and connector brokers, Pack registry, runtime pools, audit and retention, dedicated or sovereign deployment, administrative support, SLA, and long-term maintenance.

### OEM and branded editions

Pay for branded distributions, preconfigured Snacks/apps/Packs/models/policies, embedded vertical workflows, compatibility rights, and support.

## Free personal edition

The intended free baseline includes desktop shell, local object space, portable Snack, personal Graph features, local or user-funded models, basic Pack execution, import/export, local receipts, and self-hosting/fork rights subject to the final license.

Hosted storage, managed inference, or third-party services may carry their actual usage cost without changing the free software principle.

## Commercial moat

The moat is the accumulated trust system: Pack standards, vendor relationships, verification corpus, policy templates, runtime compatibility knowledge, signed registries, enterprise deployment expertise, audit evidence, protocol brand/certification, and a consumer environment vendors want to reach.

## Anti-patterns

Gummy should not become a pay-to-play application pyramid, advertising identity graph, captive model storefront, marketplace that prevents direct vendor relationships, engagement-only social feed, or cloud computer that holds export hostage.
