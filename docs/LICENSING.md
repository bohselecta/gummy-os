# Gummy OS Licensing Strategy

## Status

No final public license is granted yet. All rights remain reserved until counsel reviews the protocol, community core, commercial modules, trademark, and patent posture.

## Desired outcome

- Personal users can run and modify Gummy OS.
- Developers can create protocol-compatible independent editions, Actors, Molds, Gummies, Bowls, Links, and Grabs.
- The protocol can be implemented broadly.
- Hosted providers cannot take the whole system, close every improvement, erase attribution, or strip provenance without consequence.
- Commercial vendors can license enterprise, certification, registry, and integration technology clearly.
- Gummy OS trademarks and official compatibility marks remain controlled.

## Candidate structure

### Open specifications

Protocol schemas and interoperability documentation under a permissive specification license or covenant.

### Community core

Potentially MPL-2.0 or AGPLv3, depending desired file-level versus network-copyleft behavior and adoption friction.

### Commercial modules

Separate license for enterprise control plane, managed brokers, Actor/Mold verification services, certification tooling, signed registry infrastructure, dedicated deployment automation, compliance modules, and OEM redistribution.

### Trademark policy

Independent editions may describe themselves as protocol-compatible but cannot impersonate the official Gummy OS service, use certification marks without permission, or create confusing branding.

### Gummy and Grab rights

A Gummy may carry its own license or usage terms. A Grab must preserve the source's required attribution, provenance, and restrictions. The ability to Grab a Gummy is a protocol permission, not automatic permission to violate copyright, publicity, character, trademark, privacy, contractual, or organizational rights.

### Actor and Mold rights

A Mold may contain verified identity, organization, performer, public-figure, or character assertions. Gummy OS must not treat appearance as proof. Official and licensed assertions require a valid proof and policy path.

## Decision gates

1. complete copyright inventory;
2. identify third-party runtime licenses;
3. choose contributor agreement or developer-certificate policy;
4. define patent posture;
5. define compatibility mark;
6. define hosted-service rights;
7. define commercial exception language;
8. define Actor/Mold identity and publicity-rights posture;
9. define Grab provenance and license-carrying rules;
10. receive attorney review.

Do not paste an off-the-shelf license merely to appear open source.
