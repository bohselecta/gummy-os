# Gummy Licensing Strategy

## Status

No final public license is granted yet. All rights remain reserved until counsel reviews the protocol/core/commercial split.

## Desired outcome

- Personal users can run and modify Gummy.
- Developers can create protocol-compatible forks and Snacks.
- The protocol can be implemented broadly.
- Hosted providers cannot take the whole system, close every improvement, and erase attribution without consequence.
- Commercial vendors can license enterprise, certification, registry, and integration technology clearly.
- Gummy trademarks and official compatibility marks remain controlled.

## Candidate structure

### Open specifications

Protocol schemas and interoperability documentation under a permissive specification license or covenant.

### Community core

Potentially MPL-2.0 or AGPLv3, depending desired file-level versus network-copyleft behavior and adoption friction.

### Commercial modules

Separate license for enterprise control plane, managed brokers, certification tooling, signed registry infrastructure, dedicated deployment automation, compliance modules, and OEM redistribution.

### Trademark policy

Forks may describe themselves as protocol-compatible but cannot impersonate the official Gummy service, use certification marks, or create confusing branding.

## Decision gates

1. complete copyright inventory;
2. identify third-party runtime licenses;
3. choose contributor agreement or developer certificate policy;
4. define patent posture;
5. define compatibility mark;
6. define hosted-service rights;
7. define commercial exception language;
8. receive attorney review.

Do not paste an off-the-shelf license merely to appear open source.
