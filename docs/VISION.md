# Gummy Vision

## The proposition

A person should be able to open a URL, enter full screen, and arrive inside a complete personal computer where files, projects, conversations, applications, connected services, people, agents, and shared work are understandable as one place.

Gummy keeps the desktop metaphor because it is an adoption advantage, not a limitation. People already understand folders, windows, applications, dragging, sharing, and trash. The architectural revolution happens below that familiar surface:

- models are replaceable operators;
- authority is temporary and scoped;
- complex software can arrive with agent-operating knowledge;
- execution occurs in isolated capsules;
- social relationships are portable protocol objects;
- consequential work produces evidence;
- organizations can impose policy without replacing the consumer system.

## Product identity

**Gummy** is the product and protocol family.

**mygum.my** is the canonical personal address.

A user's **Snack** is their portable Gummy identity. A company, community, school, family, or creative group can distribute a branded Gummy edition while remaining compatible with the protocol.

## Opportunity

The consumer product can be free because the commercial product is not access to a folder icon. The licensable value is the governed meeting layer between intelligence and complicated software:

- Application Pack design and certification;
- model and connector brokers;
- enterprise policy and audit;
- verified runtime pools;
- private deployment;
- regulated workflow packs;
- OEM and embedded Gummy editions;
- cross-version compatibility and support.

## Terminal reality

A person opens Gummy.

Their computer is already there. Their Snack identifies them. Their companion understands the environment. Their files, projects, Bowls, Drops, applications, and connected resources remain where they left them.

They can ask an agent to use a complicated vendor application. The application is opened in the appropriate runtime. The agent knows how to operate it because its vendor shipped an authoritative Application Pack. Gummy grants only the required capabilities, shows approval where needed, returns the result, and records an Action Receipt.

The same person can share the result into a private Bowl, publish a public Drop, or fork another person's shared artifact into an independent edition. Gummy does not need to own their model, their identity, or every server involved. It only needs to preserve the protocol and the boundaries.
